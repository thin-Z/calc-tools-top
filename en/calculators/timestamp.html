What is a Unix Timestamp?

A Unix timestamp (or Unix time / Epoch) is the number of seconds that have elapsed since 1970-01-01 00:00:00 UTC (ignoring leap seconds). It is a universal, timezone-free way to represent an instant in time, widely used in computers and databases. As precision needs grow, many systems store milliseconds (the current time multiplied by 1000). This tool automatically distinguishes second-level timestamps (around 10 digits) from millisecond-level ones (around 13 digits) and converts as needed.

How to Use This Tool

  1. Paste a second- or millisecond-level timestamp into the "Unix Timestamp" box — the tool detects the unit and shows the matching local and UTC date in real time
  2. Or pick a date and time to compute the corresponding seconds and milliseconds
  3. Use the unit selector to force "Seconds" or "Milliseconds" and inspect the same value at a different precision
  4. The result panel shows both your local timezone and UTC for cross-timezone checks

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I tell seconds from milliseconds? In 2026, the current second-level timestamp is about 1.78 billion (10 digits), while the millisecond-level value is about 1.78 trillion (13 digits). This tool defaults to a boundary of 10^11, but you can also force a unit manually.

Why do timestamps look different across timezones? A timestamp is an absolute instant and is timezone-independent. Only the human-readable representation is affected by the timezone. This tool shows local and UTC side by side so you can verify.

Is my data uploaded to a server? No. Everything runs locally in your browser; your input never leaves your device.
