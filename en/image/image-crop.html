Online Image Crop: Frame Exactly the Part You Want

This online image cropper lets you select and remove part of an image right in your browser, trimming away unwanted edges or reframing it to a specific ratio. Everything runs locally with no uploads, so it's private and gives you instant preview and instant adjustments. Whether you're cropping a social avatar or turning a landscape shot into a vertical cover, you can do it quickly.

It's ideal for content creators, e-commerce operators, and everyday users who need to extract one precise region from a larger image.

How Is Cropping Different From Resize and Compress?

Crop keeps part of the picture and discards the rest, changing composition and ratio without changing pixel density. Resize changes overall dimensions, redrawing the image proportionally or to a target width/height. Compress reduces file size while trying to keep quality high. You often use all three together: crop away what you don't need, resize to the target, then compress to optimize.

So if you just want to remove extra background, crop; if the image is the wrong size, resize; if it's too large, compress.

How to Use It

1. Upload or drag an image (JPG / PNG / WebP / BMP, up to 50MB).
2. On the left, pick a target ratio — 1:1 (square), 16:9 (landscape), 9:16 (portrait), 4:3, 3:2 — or choose "Free".
3. Drag the crop box to the region you want to keep; fine-tune its position inside the box.
4. Preview the result live below the canvas and confirm the composition.
5. Choose an export format (PNG for transparency, JPEG for photos) and download.

Common Crop Mistakes

① Don't over-crop a low-resolution image. The more you crop, the less image remains and the blurrier it gets when enlarged. For high-resolution output, start from a high-resolution source.
② Remember composition. Cropping recomposes the photo; placing the subject a third of the way across (the rule of thirds) often looks livelier than dead center.
③ Match the platform's ratio. Different platforms require different cover/avatar specs (e.g. a 2.35:1 cover or a 1:1 avatar), so picking the platform's ratio saves effort.

How to Choose an Export Format

If the cropped image needs a transparent background (like a PNG icon or cutout), use PNG; if it's a photo or for web display where you want a smaller file, use JPEG. After cropping, if you want an even smaller file, follow up with our Image Compressor to balance clarity and loading speed.
