What is a Normal BMI? Complete Guide

BMI (Body Mass Index) is a quick screening number that relates your weight to your height. Understanding the normal range helps you read your health status and know when a number is just a number.

How BMI Is Calculated

BMI equals weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in meters. The same formula works in imperial units when you use the tool's unit switch, so you never have to convert manually.

The Normal Range

For adults, under 18.5 is underweight, 18.5-23.9 is healthy, 24-27.9 is overweight in many Asian guidelines, and 28+ is obese. Western cutoffs place the healthy band at 18.5-24.9 and obesity at 30+.

Limitations to Keep in Mind

BMI doesn't distinguish muscle from fat. Athletes can show a high BMI while carrying little body fat, and older adults can be "normal" yet have low muscle mass. Use it as a screening tool, not a diagnosis.

What to Do with Your Number

If your BMI sits outside the healthy band, focus on sustainable habits—balanced meals, regular movement, and sleep—and check with a professional before aggressive dieting.
