Why "ideal weight" is a range, not a number

Two people of the same height can both be at a healthy weight 15 kg apart. That is not a formula getting it wrong - it is because ideal weight is a range rather than a value. It depends on which formula you use, which reference standard you follow, and your own muscle mass and frame. This calculator shows three widely used formulas plus the healthy BMI band, so you can see where your weight actually sits instead of chasing a single number.

The three formulas this calculator uses

FormulaCalculationCharacterTypical use
BrocaMen: height − 100; women: height − 105Oldest and crudest; ignores build entirelyQuick self-check
BMI 22 conversion22 × height² (m)Uses the midpoint of the healthy BMI band, so it lines up with BMI categoriesComparing with BMI guidance
DevineMen: 50 + 0.9 × (height − 152); women: 45.5 + 0.9 × (height − 152)Published in 1974 for calculating drug doses; drifts further at extreme heightsClinical and pharmacy reference

How to use it: the three formulas disagreeing is normal. For a man of 170 cm they give 70.0 kg, 63.6 kg and 66.2 kg respectively - a 6.4 kg spread. Treat the BMI conversion as a sensible midpoint and the healthy range as the boundary, rather than treating any single formula as the answer. You may also see Robinson, Miller and Hamwi quoted elsewhere; they are further variants of the same idea and are not calculated here.

BMI categories

The healthy range in this calculator's result panel runs from 18.5 to 24.9, which follows the World Health Organization cut-offs:

CategoryBMI
UnderweightBelow 18.5
Normal weight18.5 – 24.9
Overweight25.0 – 29.9
Obese30.0 and above

Cut-offs are not universal. China's national standard for adults (WS/T 428-2013) is stricter: normal weight runs to 24.0, overweight starts at 24.0, and obesity at 28.0. Several other countries apply their own thresholds for Asian populations on the grounds that health risk rises at a lower BMI. When you compare yourself against a country's health guidance, use that country's cut-offs - not the WHO table alone.

Waist circumference: the measurement BMI misses

Weight alone cannot tell you where fat sits, and fat stored around the abdomen carries more risk than fat stored elsewhere. China's standard uses waist circumference alongside BMI:

MeasureMenWomen
Central obesity, at risk85 – 89.9 cm80 – 84.9 cm
Central obesity90 cm and above85 cm and above

How to use it: measure at the level of the navel, at the end of a normal breath out, without pulling the tape tight. If your BMI is inside the normal band but your waist crosses these thresholds, the weight figure is telling you less than the tape measure is - and losing a few centimetres of waist will do more for your risk profile than losing the same kilograms elsewhere.

Average is not ideal

Population averages are useful precisely because they show why "average" is a poor personal target. Measured data from the US CDC's National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) puts American adults at roughly:

MeasureValueWhat it implies
Average height, adult menabout 175.3 cm (5'9")Reference point only
Average weight, adult menabout 90.8 kgBMI of roughly 29.6 - in the overweight band
Average waist, adult menabout 103.9 cmWell past the 90 cm risk threshold

How to use it: the average adult man's weight works out to a BMI high in the overweight range, and his waist measurement is above the level associated with increased risk. Matching the average is therefore not a health goal. Set targets from BMI and waist thresholds, not from what other people weigh.

Turning the result into a target you can act on

Check things in this order. First, where does your BMI fall - under 18.5, 18.5 to 24.9, or 25 and above? Second, does your waist cross the threshold for your sex? Third, if your weight looks normal but your waist does not, look at body fat rather than the scale.

The pace matters as much as the target. China's national health guidance recommends losing no more than about 0.5 kg per week and aiming to lose 5% to 10% of body weight over 3 to 6 months. For someone at 80 kg that means 4 to 8 kg over several months - slower than most crash diets promise, but far more likely to stay off. If you are underweight, rule out underlying illness first, then increase energy and protein intake gradually while adding resistance training, so that what you gain is muscle rather than fat.

One practical detail: body weight fluctuates by 1 to 2 kg within a day because of food, fluid, salt and (for women) the menstrual cycle. Weigh yourself at the same time of day, under the same conditions, once a week, and judge the trend rather than any single reading.

Three worked examples

Example 1 (defaults): a man of 170 cm. The tool gives Broca 70.0 kg, BMI 22 conversion 63.6 kg and Devine 66.2 kg, with a healthy range of 53.5 – 72.0 kg.

Example 2 (a woman of 160 cm): Broca 55.0 kg, BMI 22 conversion 56.3 kg, Devine 52.7 kg, healthy range 47.4 – 63.7 kg. Notice that Broca now gives a lower figure than the BMI conversion - the opposite of the pattern for men, because the formula subtracts 105 rather than 100 from a woman's height.

Example 3 (a tall man of 185 cm): Broca 85.0 kg, BMI 22 conversion 75.3 kg, Devine 79.7 kg, healthy range 63.3 – 85.2 kg. Going from 170 cm to 185 cm adds 15.0 kg under Broca but only 11.7 kg under the BMI conversion, because Broca scales linearly while BMI scales with the square of height. That growing gap is exactly why Broca is treated as a rough check rather than a standard. More questions about ideal weight

Which formula is the most accurate? None of them is definitive, because they all reduce a person to a height. For everyday use, work from the healthy BMI range and treat Broca and Devine as cross-checks. For anything clinical - medication dosing in particular - follow the formula your clinician uses rather than converting one yourself.

Is weight within the healthy range the same as being healthy? No. BMI cannot distinguish muscle from fat and says nothing about where fat is stored. Normal weight with a high waist measurement or high body fat carries real risk, which is why three measurements beat one.

What if I lift weights and my BMI says overweight? Muscle is denser than fat, so trained people often run a BMI above 25 without excess fat. In that case the BMI figure loses much of its meaning: judge by waist circumference and body fat percentage, and do not cut weight just to move a number.

Does age change the healthy weight? Yes. Muscle mass declines with age while fat proportion rises, so the same weight means something different at 50 than at 25. For older adults, an aggressive low weight is a worse goal than maintaining muscle and keeping the waist in check. The age field in this tool is recorded but does not enter any of the three formulas.

How fast should weight change? Slowly: about 0.5 kg a week at most, with a 5% to 10% reduction over 3 to 6 months. Faster loss is usually water and muscle, and it tends to come back.
