How to Calculate Discounts: Formula and Tips

Discount math is everywhere—sales, coupons, and price negotiations. A small calculation mistake can make you think you're saving more than you are.

The Basic Discount Formula

Final price equals original price times (1 minus the discount rate). A 20% discount on $80 means paying 80% of $80, or $64. Work in the percentage you pay, not the percentage off.

Stacked Discounts

Two 10% discounts are not 20%. The second discount applies to the already-reduced price, so 10% then 10% totals only 19% off.

What You Actually Pay

Focus on the final price and the after-tax total. A heavily discounted item with high shipping can cost more than a smaller discount with free delivery.

Comparing Deals

Convert each deal to a price per unit or a percentage off the same baseline. Then the best value becomes obvious without guesswork.

Discounts and Sales Tax

Remember that most discounts apply before sales tax is added. A 30% discount on an item still carries tax on the reduced price, so the final checkout total may differ from your quick mental math.

When comparing prices across regions, always compare the after-tax totals. Some markets show tax-inclusive prices and others do not, which can flip which deal is actually cheaper.

How to Calculate a Discount

  3. Add tax or shipping if you want the true out-of-pocket total.

For stacked promotions, enter the discounts in sequence—the second applies to the first result, not the original price.

Quick tip: when a store advertises 'up to 50% off', always calculate the actual item you want. Clearance signs often mix tiers, and only the final price tells you if the deal is real. The final price is the only number that counts when you are at the register.
