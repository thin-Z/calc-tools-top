Loan Comparison Calculator: Compare Different Loan Plans

Comparing loans on the monthly payment alone hides the real cost. The right comparison looks at total interest, fees, term, and flexibility before you sign.

Compare APR, Not Just Rate

The APR bundles the interest rate with origination and service fees into one comparable number. A loan with a lower headline rate but high fees can cost more than a higher-rate, low-fee alternative.

Term Length Trade-offs

Shorter terms mean higher payments but far less total interest. Longer terms ease monthly cash flow at the price of thousands in extra interest over the life of the loan.

Prepayment and Flexibility

Check whether the lender allows extra payments without penalty. The ability to prepay turns a long loan into a flexible one and lets you capture interest savings whenever cash allows.

Build a Side-by-Side Table

List each offer's APR, term, monthly payment, and total repaid. The lowest total repaid is usually the cheapest, provided the monthly payment fits your budget.

Hidden Fees to Watch

Beyond the APR, watch for application fees, early settlement penalties, and late-payment charges. A loan with a slightly higher rate but no origination fee can win on total cost.

Read the fine print for mandatory add-ons like insurance or account-keeping fees. These change the effective cost and are often negotiable or avoidable.

How to Compare Loan Offers

  1. Enter each offer's amount, rate, fees, and term into the comparison.
  2. Compare the monthly payment and total repaid side by side.
  3. Pick the lowest total cost that still fits your monthly budget.

Include all fees in the comparison—an offer that looks cheap on the headline rate can lose once origination and service fees are added.

Quick tip: run every offer through the same assumptions. The moment you standardize term, fees, and total cost, the 'cheap' loan often stops looking cheap.
