What is Compound Interest? A Complete Guide

Compound interest is what makes long-term saving and investing powerful—and what makes debt dangerous. It is interest earned on interest, so balances grow faster the longer they compound.

The Core Formula

The future value equals the principal times (1 + rate per period) raised to the number of periods. The more frequently interest compounds—daily versus annually—the higher the final amount, all else equal.

Why Frequency Matters

Compounding monthly instead of yearly adds extra growth cycles each year. Over decades this gap becomes large, which is why high-yield savings and dividend reinvestment beat simple-interest accounts.

Compounding in Debt

Credit cards compound daily, so unpaid balances explode. Paying more than the minimum sharply cuts the interest you owe because you shrink the balance that future interest is calculated on.

The Rule of 72

Divide 72 by your annual return to estimate doubling time. At 6% you double in about 12 years; at 9% in 8. The same rule shows how fast debt doubles if you only pay minimums.
