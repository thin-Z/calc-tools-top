Keyword Density Analyzer: Key Metric for SEO Optimization

Keyword density measures how often a target phrase appears relative to the total word count. Used well, it supports SEO; overused, it can hurt readability and rankings.

How Density Is Calculated

Density is the number of times the keyword appears divided by total words, expressed as a percentage. A 2% density means the keyword appears twice per hundred words.

The Healthy Range

Most SEO guidance suggests 1-3% density. Below that, the page may seem off-topic; above it, the text reads as stuffed and may trigger spam filters.

Density Is Not Everything

Search engines read context and intent, not just counts. Synonyms, related terms, and natural placement matter more than hitting an exact percentage.

Fix Over-Optimized Text

If density runs high, replace some keyword instances with pronouns and synonyms. Re-read the result aloud—if it sounds repetitive, rewrite.

Density Tools and How to Use Them

Keyword density checkers count occurrences and compute the percentage automatically. Paste the final draft and check the target phrase, then adjust only if the number looks unnatural.

Use the tool across multiple candidate keywords, not just one. A page can rank for dozens of related phrases, and over-optimizing a single term rarely helps overall visibility.

How to Check Keyword Density

  1. Paste the final draft of your page into the tool.
  2. Enter the primary keyword you want to measure.
  3. Review the percentage and adjust only if the text reads unnaturally.

Check two or three related phrases as well—a page that covers a topic naturally usually lands in range without forcing the keyword.

Quick tip: write for the reader first, then measure. If the density lands in range without you forcing it, the page is likely well-targeted; if you had to stuff the phrase, the structure needs rework.
