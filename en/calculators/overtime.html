Which overtime rules this calculator applies

This calculator uses the statutory overtime rules of mainland China. A monthly-paid employee's hourly rate is derived as monthly salary ÷ 21.75 ÷ 8, and overtime is then paid at 150% for work on a working day, 200% for work on a rest day that is not compensated with time off, and 300% for work on a statutory holiday. It is the right tool if you work in China, or manage payroll for staff in China, and want to check whether the overtime on a payslip meets the statutory minimum.

The mechanics are different if you are paid under US federal rules, where overtime is triggered by hours beyond 40 in a workweek rather than by the type of day worked. The comparison table further down sets out both models side by side, so you can see which one applies to you.

How to use it: if your employer offers "time off instead" for weekend work, that is allowed for rest-day overtime. If the same work falls on a statutory holiday such as 1 October, time off cannot replace the 300% payment. That distinction is the single most common point of confusion when checking a payslip.

Where 21.75 days comes from

China's Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security sets the conversion in Circular Lao She Bu Fa [2008] No. 3. A year has 365 days, 104 of which are rest days. Statutory holidays must be paid even though no work is performed (Labour Law, Article 51), so they are not deducted from the paid-day count. That gives 21.75 = (365 − 104) ÷ 12 paid days per month, and 174 paid hours when multiplied by 8 hours a day.

The same circular defines a second figure, 20.83 working days per month (250 ÷ 12), but that one measures working time — for example, when checking whether an employee on a consolidated working-hours system has exceeded the statutory total. Mixing the two up is a common error.

Note that 20.83 was calculated when there were 11 statutory holidays. Since 2025 there are 13, which strictly gives (365 − 104 − 13) ÷ 12 or about 20.67 days, although the circular has not been updated. The 21.75 figure is unaffected, because its numerator only removes rest days.

The remaining days of each holiday period — for example 15 February and 20–23 February, or 4–7 October — are rest days: your employer may arrange time off, and if none is arranged the rate is 200%. Within a single holiday period, different dates therefore carry different rates.

Overtime is capped at 36 hours a month

Article 41 of the Labour Law limits overtime to one hour a day, three hours in special circumstances, and 36 hours in any month. Put those 36 hours into different day types and the pay differs sharply — here at a $5,000 monthly salary:

How to use it: the same number of hours can be worth twice as much depending on when they were worked. If the overtime on a payslip looks low, check first whether all hours were recorded (including remote and on-call work), then whether the hourly base was calculated from the contractual salary rather than the local minimum wage.

Three worked examples

Example 1 (working-day overtime): a $5,000 monthly salary with 10 hours of weekday overtime. The tool reports an hourly rate of $28.74 and overtime of $431.03 (hourly rate × 1.5 × 10 hours), for total monthly pay of about $5,431.03.

Example 2 (National Day): the same salary, with 8 hours worked on 1, 2 and 3 October — 24 hours in total, all statutory holidays. At the 300% rate the overtime comes to $2,068.97, equal to nine days of normal pay. Had the same 24 hours fallen on 4–7 October (rest days), the amount would be $1,379.31, a difference of about $689.66 — before any time off in lieu is considered.

Example 3 (mixed): 10 hours on working days, 10 on rest days and 10 on a statutory holiday. The breakdown is $431.03 on working days, $574.71 on rest days and $862.07 on the statutory holiday, giving a total of $1,867.82. A third of the hours produce almost half the pay, which is why the dates matter more than the total hours. More questions about overtime pay

Can my employer give time off instead of paying for weekend work? For rest-day work, yes, provided equivalent time off is actually taken. For statutory holiday work, no — the 300% rate is mandatory and cannot be substituted.

My employer calculated overtime from the minimum wage. Is that allowed? The base should be the normal working-time wage agreed in the employment contract, and it cannot fall below the local minimum wage. If the contractual salary is higher than the minimum wage, using the minimum wage as the base understates the payment. Payslips, attendance records and bank statements are the evidence that matters in a dispute.

Does "voluntary" overtime have to be paid? It depends on who required it. If the employer asked, expected or effectively required the additional hours through workload, they count as overtime. If an employee stays late on their own to do unrelated work, they do not.

How does this work under a consolidated working-hours system? Working time is totalled over a month, quarter or year and compared with the statutory total for that period; hours above it are treated as extended working time and paid at 150%. Work on a statutory holiday still attracts 300% separately. The comparison uses 20.83 days a month (about 166.64 hours), not the 21.75 days used for pay conversion.

What isn't included here? The calculator covers the three statutory multipliers only. It excludes local allowances such as night-shift or heat allowances, unused annual leave payouts, social insurance and income tax, and minimum-wage compliance checks. Treat the result as a check on the statutory minimum rather than a final payroll figure.
