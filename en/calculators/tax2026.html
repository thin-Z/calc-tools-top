The Tax Calculator 2026 applies China's current Individual Income Tax Law to your pay. How the tax is calculated (four steps)

Step 1: subtract the 5,000 yuan monthly threshold. Step 2: subtract the social insurance and housing fund you pay personally — the law calls this "special deductions". Step 3: subtract your special additional deductions (children's education, housing loan interest, rent, elderly care and more). What remains is your taxable income. Step 4: annualise it (× 12) and apply the progressive brackets below. Because the system is progressive, only the slice of income that crosses into a higher band is taxed at that band's rate — a 20% top band never means your whole salary is taxed at 20%. Common myth: is separate taxation of the year-end bonus always better?

Not always. Separate taxation divides the bonus by 12 to find a rate and taxes the bonus on its own; combining it with comprehensive income adds the bonus to your salary and applies the annual table. When your salary alone already reaches a 20%–45% marginal band, folding the bonus in can push it into a higher rate; when your salary is modest and the bonus is your main income, separate taxation usually wins. The 2026 filing still lets you choose one of the two, so try both and keep the cheaper one. Bear in mind that the "separate" option can be used only once per year across all bonuses. Tax Calculator 2026 FAQ

What is the difference between the 5,000 yuan threshold, "special deductions" and "special additional deductions"?

They stack in layers. The threshold (basic deduction) is a flat 5,000 yuan subtracted every month for everyone. Special deductions are the social insurance (pension 8%, medical 2%, unemployment 0.5%, and so on) and housing fund (5%–12%) you pay personally, which are deducted pre-tax. Special additional deductions cover seven categories of livelihood expenses such as children's education, mortgage interest and elderly care, and you claim them yourself in the official tax app. All three come off your income before tax is worked out — which is why take-home falls by more than the tax alone, since insurance and fund come off too.

Can my personal social insurance and housing fund contributions really be deducted pre-tax?

Yes. The law states that the basic pension, basic medical insurance, unemployment insurance and housing fund you pay (up to the local ceiling) qualify as "special deductions" and are subtracted in full when computing taxable income — they are not treated as taxable income. That means the housing fund portion of your pay both lands in your own account and stays out of your taxable income, a genuine double benefit. It is also why people in high-contribution cities can show a high pre-tax salary yet a comparatively low tax burden.

Is separate taxation of the year-end bonus better than combining it into comprehensive income?

There is no single answer — it depends on your salary band. Separate taxation divides the bonus by 12 to locate a rate and taxes it alone, which suits people whose bonus is large relative to salary. Combining it treats the bonus as part of annual comprehensive income at the yearly table, which suits people whose salary is already high enough that folding the bonus in would push it into a higher marginal rate. The 2026 filing still allows either option, so compare both and pick the lower tax. Remember the "separate" option can only be used once a year across all bonuses.

What special additional deductions exist, and how much can each save in 2026?

There are seven: children's education at 2,000 yuan per child per month; infant and toddler care under age 3 at 2,000 yuan per child per month; continuing education at 400 yuan per month (academic) or 3,600 yuan per year (professional qualification); housing loan interest at 1,000 yuan per month (first home); housing rent at 800–1,500 yuan per month depending on city; elderly care at 3,000 yuan per month (only child) or shared among siblings; and major medical expenses, deductible on the portion above 15,000 yuan of out-of-pocket cost last year, capped at 80,000 yuan per year. Several can be combined and split between spouses, so filing them properly can meaningfully lower your tax.

Why do comprehensive income, labour remuneration and business income use different rates?

Comprehensive income (salaries and wages plus labour remuneration, author's remuneration and royalties combined) uses the 3%–45% seven-band annual table. Labour remuneration is withheld separately at 20%–40% on three tiers and then merged into comprehensive income at filing. Business income (sole proprietors, contractors) uses a separate 5%–35% five-band table and may deduct costs. The same money can attract quite different rates and deductions depending on which category it falls into — which is why freelancers and part-timers must know their status. This tool targets the most common case: salaried comprehensive income.

Why does my take-home pay differ between January and December under the cumulative withholding method?

Salaries and wages use the "cumulative withholding method": each month's withholding = (cumulative income − cumulative deductions) × rate − quick deduction − tax already withheld. Early in the year cumulative income is low, so you sit in a low band, withhold less and take home more. As income accumulates the band may rise, so from mid-year onward the monthly withholding grows and take-home falls. Your total tax for the year does not change — the difference is settled with a refund or top-up during the annual reconciliation in March–June of the following year.
