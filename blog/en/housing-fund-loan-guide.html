Housing Fund Loan Guide 2026: Limits, Balances and City Comparison

A housing provident fund loan (住房公积金贷款) is the cheapest money most home buyers in China can borrow. In 2026 the five-year-plus rate for a first home is 2.6%, while the five-year-plus LPR sits at 3.5% and first-home commercial loans are actually written at roughly 3.05% (LPR minus 45 basis points). On a 1,000,000 CNY loan repaid over 30 years, that gap is worth about 86,000 CNY in interest.

The catch is that a fund loan is not a blank cheque. Your final amount is the smallest of four separate constraints: the local policy cap, your account balance multiplied by the local multiplier, your contribution base (repayment capacity test), and the property-value limit. This guide uses 2026 public policy figures to lay out the arithmetic, the city-by-city caps, the rate gap against commercial lending, and the misconceptions that cost borrowers money.

2. The Four Constraints That Cap Your Loan

Eligible borrowers often assume the cap is the only number that matters. In practice four tests run at the same time and the lowest one wins:

  • Policy cap: the maximum amount the local fund centre allows. Major cities raised these substantially in 2026.
  • Balance multiplier: most cities use "account balance × N". Shenzhen uses 16×, Hangzhou 20×, Jinan and Chengdu 25×, Qingdao 15×. Guangzhou instead uses "balance × 8 + monthly contribution × months to retirement".
  • Contribution base: Shenzhen applies "contribution base ÷ 2 ÷ payment coefficient". A low base can block you even when your balance is large.
  • Property-value limit: the loan cannot exceed the property price minus the minimum down payment, typically 20% for a first home.

Your borrowable amount is the smallest of those four. This is where most surprises come from: borrowers watch the balance and ignore the base and the cap.

3. 2026 Major-City Limits at a Glance

Shanghai, Shenzhen, Guangzhou and Beijing all loosened fund policy during 2026, and every one of them led with a higher cap. The difference lies in the uplift conditions: Beijing and Shanghai stayed relatively conservative, while Shenzhen and Guangzhou attached much more generous family-based uplifts.

Notice that the gap between cities is not in the base amount but in the uplift conditions. Guangzhou's "+40% for a second child, +50% for a third" and Shenzhen's "+50% for first marriage and first birth" mean that two families buying the same flat with identical contributions can end up more than 1,000,000 CNY apart. 6. Three Worked Examples

All three examples assume a 30-year equal-installment repayment at the 2.6% first-home fund rate.

Example 1: a Beijing family borrowing 1,200,000 CNY. Both spouses have contributed for two years and hold 90,000 CNY combined. Beijing's cap and the balance test both permit the full amount, so the loan goes through at 1,200,000 CNY. The monthly payment is about 4,804 CNY with roughly 529,000 CNY of interest over 30 years. The same amount on a 3.05% commercial loan would cost about 5,091 CNY a month and 633,000 CNY in interest, so the fund loan saves about 287 CNY a month and roughly 104,000 CNY overall.

Example 2: a Shenzhen first-home family with 80,000 CNY in the account. At 16×, the balance supports about 1,280,000 CNY, which is below the 1,300,000 CNY family base cap. The balance test therefore becomes the binding constraint. Reaching the base cap needs about 81,000 CNY; reaching the 2,990,000 CNY uplifted amount needs about 187,000 CNY. A generous policy cap is useless if the balance cannot support it.

Example 3: a Guangzhou family with two children buying a certified "good home". The 2,000,000 CNY base rises by 40% for the second child (800,000 CNY) and 30% for the good-home certification (600,000 CNY), giving a theoretical maximum near 3,400,000 CNY, below Guangzhou's 3,600,000 CNY ceiling. But if the account holds only 100,000 CNY, the ×8 test supports just 800,000 CNY and the final offer collapses. Policy sets the ceiling; the account sets reality.

7. Three Common Misconceptions

Misconception 1: a bigger balance always means a bigger loan. The balance is only one of four tests. A low contribution base, a low policy cap or the property-value limit can each pull the number down, and the lowest test wins.

Misconception 2: withdrawing the balance is harmless because you can rebuild it. Withdrawals reduce the balance directly, and most cities size the loan from that balance. Withdrawing 50,000 CNY removes roughly 800,000 CNY of borrowing capacity at 16×, and rebuilding it takes years.

Misconception 3: always take the longest term. A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest. On 1,000,000 CNY at 2.6%: a 10-year term costs about 9,473 CNY a month and roughly 137,000 CNY in interest, while a 30-year term costs about 4,003 CNY a month and roughly 441,000 CNY in interest. The right choice follows your cash flow, not the lowest possible payment. For the long-run mechanics of principal and interest, see equal installment vs equal principal.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Can both spouses use a fund loan at the same time?

Yes. Combined applications raise the ceiling. In 2026 Beijing's family base amount is 2,400,000 CNY, Guangzhou's is 2,000,000 CNY and Shenzhen's is 1,300,000 CNY, but both spouses must satisfy the contribution requirements.

Can I use a fund loan for a second home?

Yes, but the rate rises to 3.075% instead of 2.6%, and the amount is usually lower than for a first home. Rate movements across cities are covered in 2026 mortgage rate trends.

Is a combined loan always a bad deal?

No. When the fund cap cannot cover the purchase, topping up with a commercial loan is sensible, because the fund portion still earns the 2.6% rate and the blended cost stays below an all-commercial loan.

What if I move to another city?

Most cities no longer restrict applicants by household registration or contribution location. As long as you meet the continuous contribution requirement where you apply, the local multiplier and cap rules apply, and cross-province transfers require merging the accounts first.

How long must I contribute before applying?

Regular employees generally need six consecutive months of full contributions. Flexible-employment contributors need twelve consecutive months and three years of cumulative contributions.

9. Three Practical Tips

  • Check your contribution status early: a break resets the consecutive-month count. Avoid resigning or pausing contributions in the months before a purchase.
  • Confirm the local multiplier before saving: multipliers range from 8× to 25×, so the same balance unlocks very different amounts depending on the city. Confirm the target city's rule first, then decide how long to build the balance.
  • Model the whole term, not just the first year: the fund advantage compounds over decades. For long-horizon planning beyond the mortgage itself, see compound interest explained.
