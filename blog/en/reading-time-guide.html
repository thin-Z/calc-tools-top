Reading Time Calculator: Estimate Article Reading and Speaking Time

A reading time estimate tells readers how long an article takes to finish—and tells writers whether a piece is the right length for its audience.

How Reading Time Is Estimated

Most calculators divide the word count by an average reading speed, usually 200-250 words per minute for English. Chinese content is often estimated by characters per minute instead.

Why It Matters for Content

Readers decide early whether an article is worth their time. Showing a short reading time improves click-through on long-form posts, while honest estimates set expectations.

Adjusting the Speed

Technical or dense material is read more slowly than casual prose. If your article is jargon-heavy, use a slower speed in the estimate to stay realistic.

Using the Number in Planning

Match article length to intent: quick answers in 1-3 minutes, detailed guides in 5-10. The counter helps you hit those targets consistently.

Reading Time in Publishing Platforms

Many content platforms display estimated reading time automatically. Since they use a fixed speed, a long technical article can show a shorter estimate than it truly takes to absorb.

Use a counter with adjustable speed to sanity-check the platform's number, and keep dense sections broken into scannable headings to reduce actual reading friction.

How to Estimate Reading Time

  1. Paste the article text into the counter.
  2. Set a reading speed that matches the audience and difficulty.
  3. Use the minutes estimate to label the post or plan its length.

For mixed-language text, confirm which language mode the tool uses—Chinese and English are estimated very differently.

Quick tip: keep a two-to-three-minute version of long articles. Many readers skim first and commit later, and a short summary dramatically lifts completion rates. Busy readers decide fast, so make the skim easy and the payoff obvious from the first lines.
