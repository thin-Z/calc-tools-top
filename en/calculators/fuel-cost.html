About the Fuel Cost Calculator

The Fuel Cost Calculator is a completely free online tool that turns "distance driven" into "fuel money spent". Enter distance, fuel consumption per 100 km and the pump price, and you get total fuel used, total cost and cost per kilometer — all computed locally in your browser, with no network request and nothing uploaded. Daily drivers, long-haul truckers and anyone costing a single vehicle can use it for a quick estimate.

How the fuel cost is calculated

The core idea is to convert distance into fuel volume first, then multiply by price:

  • Total fuel used = distance ÷ 100 × fuel per 100 km
  • Total cost = total fuel used × price per liter
  • Cost per km = total cost ÷ distance

One point that is easy to miss: combining the three formulas gives cost per km = fuel per 100 km × price per liter ÷ 100 — independent of the distance driven. For the same car at the same price, driving 100 km or 1,000 km gives exactly the same cost per kilometer; only the total bill changes. So when judging whether a car is expensive to run, the cost per kilometer is the only number you need to compare — distance just decides how much you pay in total.

How to measure your real fuel consumption

Official figures come from a laboratory test cycle and rarely match real use. To get a reliable input, use the fill-to-click method:

  1. Fill the tank and stop when the nozzle clicks off — do not round up to a whole amount.
  2. Reset the trip meter (or note the current odometer reading).
  3. Drive normally for a while, ideally at least 300 km; shorter runs carry more error.
  4. Fill again until it clicks off, and record liters added and km driven.
  5. Fuel per 100 km = liters ÷ km × 100. Average two or three runs for a stable figure.

If the result is clearly high, check tire pressure, a clogged air filter, carbon deposits and a pattern of short low-speed trips first — those are the most common causes of a real-world rise in consumption.

Four worked examples

  1. Tool defaults: 100 km, 8 L/100 km, $1.00/L → 8.0 L used, $8.00 total, $0.08 per km.
  2. Highway trip: 500 km, 7 L/100 km, $1.10/L → 35.0 L, $38.50, $0.077 per km — steady-speed driving uses less fuel, so even at a higher price the per-km cost beats city driving.
  3. A month of commuting: 40 km × 22 working days = 880 km, 9 L/100 km, $1.00/L → 79.2 L, $79.20 a month, $0.09 per km.
  4. A full year: 20,000 km, 10 L/100 km, $1.20/L → 2,000 L, $2,400 a year, $0.12 per km.

Fuel Cost Calculator FAQ

How is cost per kilometer calculated?

Cost per km = fuel per 100 km × price per liter ÷ 100, independent of distance. For example 8 L/100 km at $1.00 per liter gives $0.08 per km. Multiply by your actual distance for the total bill.

How do I measure my car's real fuel consumption?

Fill until the nozzle clicks off, reset the trip meter, drive at least 300 km, then fill to the click again. Fuel per 100 km = liters ÷ km × 100. Repeat two or three times and average — one measurement carries a large error.

Fuel prices keep changing — will the result still be accurate?

The tool calculates statically from the price you enter and does not fetch live prices. Pump prices vary widely by region and change often, so enter your local station's current price. The formula itself is exact; only the price input is an estimate.

Why is my real consumption higher than the official figure?

Official figures come from a laboratory test cycle. Real driving adds congestion, stop-and-go traffic, air conditioning, short cold trips, low tire pressure and extra load, commonly 1–3 L/100 km higher. To judge whether your car has changed, measure the same commute repeatedly instead of comparing to the official number.

How do I convert L/100km to MPG?

US MPG = 235.215 ÷ (L/100 km); UK MPG = 282.481 ÷ (L/100 km). For example 8 L/100 km is about 29.4 US MPG or 35.3 UK MPG. This calculator works in liters per 100 km, so convert first if your spec sheet quotes MPG.

How to read the result

Each of the three numbers has its own use: total fuel used tells you how much to pump and whether one tank covers the trip; total cost is the actual outlay for this journey or period; and cost per kilometer is the figure for comparison — use it to weigh different cars, different driving styles, or fuel against taxis and rail on a per-passenger basis. For fleet costing, folding fuel into a per-kilometer figure is the only way to see true margin.
